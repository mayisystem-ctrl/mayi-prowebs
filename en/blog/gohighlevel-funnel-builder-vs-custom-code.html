GoHighLevel's Funnel Builder vs. a Custom-Coded Landing Page - When Each One Is Right for Your Business

Short answer: GoHighLevel's native Funnels builder is enough when the goal is to get a landing page live fast for one paid campaign, with no code involved, and you're already running GHL for automation behind the scenes anyway. A custom-coded page costs more upfront, but pays off when that page becomes a permanent business asset - because the load speed and HTML structure the builder limits you to affect both your conversion rate and your odds of being cited by an AI engine. The simple test: a page you'll build once for a campaign and never touch again - the builder is enough. A page that will keep getting organic traffic and updates for a year or more - custom code wins.

Why this is even a question, not just a budget call

Both pages look similar to a visitor - a headline, a promise, a lead form, a call-to-action button. But at the code level they're fundamentally different outputs. A page built in GoHighLevel's Funnels is assembled from the builder's ready-made blocks, and loads the builder's rendering engine plus the CSS and JS library shared across the entire builder - even if this specific page only uses a fraction of it. A custom-coded page is written for that exact page and no other, with zero extra weight. That difference is invisible to the eye, but it shows up in load speed, in how clean the HTML is, and in what search engines and AI engines can actually read and cite from the page.

When GoHighLevel's Funnels builder is the right choice

  • A one-off or short-term paid campaign - not an asset meant to live for years.
  • Your internal team needs to edit and duplicate pages on its own, without going back to a developer for every small change.
  • The page is part of a multi-step funnel wired directly into a CRM pipeline and automation, where keeping it inside the builder makes that wiring simpler.
  • The budget simply doesn't justify custom development for a page that will be replaced in a couple of months.

In most of these cases, the Funnels builder is the right, cheap choice - and setting that expectation correctly is actually part of how we scope a GHL account at May I: not every page needs to be a development project.

Where custom code wins - speed, and the conversion that follows it

Every extra second of load time hurts conversion sharply. A Portent study of e-commerce pages found the average conversion rate sits at 3.05% on a page that loads in one second, and drops to just 0.67% on a page that takes 4 seconds to load (source: portent.com). A builder-based page ships with a fixed baseline weight - the rendering engine, helper libraries, and scripts for every builder component - regardless of how many of them this particular page actually uses. A custom-coded page loads only what it needs. And since June 2021, page speed (Core Web Vitals) has also been an official Google ranking factor, not just a user-experience nicety (source: developers.google.com).

Where custom code wins - content structure and AI engine visibility

Pages built inside a sales-funnel builder are usually one-dimensional scroll pages - big headlines baked into images, visual sections, and thin running text. That works fine for a paid campaign, but it's weak for AI engines that look for a direct, structured answer to cite. 44.2% of ChatGPT's citations come from the first 30% of a document - so even when text exists on the page, if it's buried under visual sections or arrives too late, the AI engine simply never reaches it (source: searchengineland.com). A custom-coded page lets you build a real hierarchical HTML structure - a direct-answer opening, properly ordered H2 headings, full paragraphs - that both Google and AI engines can actually parse and cite from.

The hybrid option - a custom-coded page, with GHL running everything behind it

Most of the clients we work with at May I don't choose between the two options - they combine them. The landing page itself is built in custom code, with the speed and structure described above, and its form is connected to GoHighLevel through a webhook or API. The lead enters the pipeline, gets tagged, and enters the exact same automation as it would if the page had been built inside the builder itself. That way you get both the speed and content quality of a custom-coded page, and the full CRM and automation layer of GHL.

The common mistake - picking the wrong tool for the stage

The first direction: building a fully custom-coded landing page for a two-week campaign - an investment that won't pay for itself in time. The second, more common direction: running the business's central digital asset - the page meant to drive organic traffic and get cited by AI engines for a year or more - as a page inside a sales-funnel builder, and only finding out later that it can't compete on speed or structure with custom-coded sites in Google ranking or AI citations.
