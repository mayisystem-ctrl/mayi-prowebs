Website Speed & Core Web Vitals - Why Every Second Costs You Customers

Short answer: a joint Google and Deloitte study of 37 brand sites and over 30 million sessions found that just a 0.1-second improvement in mobile load time raised conversions by 8.4% in retail and 10.1% in travel (source: web.dev). That's not a theoretical statistic - it's why Google turned load speed into an official ranking factor through Core Web Vitals, and why custom code almost always beats page builders and pre-made templates when performance is on the line.

The three numbers Google actually measures

Core Web Vitals are three concrete metrics, not a vague sense of "fast" or "slow":

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) - how long until the main content on the page has loaded. Target: under 2.5 seconds.
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint) - how long the page takes to respond to a click or tap. Target: under 200 milliseconds.
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) - how much elements "jump" while loading. Target: under 0.1.

These three metrics (source: web.dev) have been an official part of Google's ranking algorithm since 2021 - meaning a site that misses them doesn't just lose visitors directly, it also starts the race for a spot in Google at a disadvantage.

How this translates to money in practice

Beyond the Google and Deloitte study, a separate study by agency Portent that examined over 100 million pageviews across 20 business sites found that a site loading in one second converts three times higher than a site loading in five seconds (source: portent.com). This lines up with plain logic: a potential customer who waits too long loses the intent to act, and sometimes closes the tab before the page even finishes loading.

The problem is most business owners never see this as a number on a screen - they see it as "fewer inquiries than expected," without knowing why. Load speed is one of the few factors you can measure precisely, fix, and see the result of within weeks.

Why page builders and pre-made templates fail here

WordPress with a heavy theme, Wix, or any drag-and-drop builder isn't necessarily efficient with code. Every plugin, every pre-styled template, and every "nice" effect installed from an extension marketplace adds JavaScript and CSS files the browser has to download and run - even if 90% of that code is irrelevant to the specific page the user is viewing. That's why builder-based sites tend to get stuck with slow LCP and high CLS, even when the design itself looks simple.

Custom code is built in the opposite direction: you start from a blank page and add only what the page actually needs. There's no translation layer between "what you want to build" and "what the builder knows how to produce." The result is typically a lighter page, with fewer network requests, that's far easier to keep under the Core Web Vitals thresholds - not because it's "more expensive," but because there's no excess code to compensate for.

What actually slows sites down

  • Unoptimized images - a 3MB original file instead of a compressed, properly sized version.
  • Third-party scripts - chat widgets, analytics, pixels - loaded before the content instead of after.
  • External fonts that block rendering until they fully load.
  • CSS/JS from plugins that are only relevant to other pages on the site, but load anyway.

None of these require "technical magic" to fix - they require someone to notice them in the first place, and that's exactly the difference between a site built with attention to performance and one assembled from ready-made pieces.

The angle most businesses miss - it affects AI too

Speed and performance don't just affect human users and Google - they also affect how easily AI engine crawlers can scan and understand a site. That connects to a broader rule: a study on ChatGPT citations found that 44.2% of citations come from the first 30% of a document (source: searchengineland.com). Meaning even a site with excellent content won't get much use out of it if an AI engine (or a human user) hits a loading delay before ever reaching that content. A fast technical foundation is a baseline requirement - not a bonus - for anyone who wants both Google ranking and AI engine visibility.

How to check this in two minutes

Google's official tool, PageSpeed Insights, gives a score and exact numbers for LCP, INP, and CLS based on real user data (when there's enough traffic) plus a lab test. It's not a tool you need to interpret - it simply tells you whether the page meets the target or not, and points to the specific cause of the delay.
