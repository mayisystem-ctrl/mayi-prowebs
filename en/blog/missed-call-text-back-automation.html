Missed Call Text-Back Automation - How GoHighLevel Catches a Lead Who Called and Got No Answer, Before They Call a Competitor

Short answer: missed-call text-back automation sends a caller who got no answer an SMS within seconds, and opens a lead card for them in the CRM - so they continue the conversation by text instead of trying the next business on the search results page. This matters because, per Invoca's Call Conversion Industry Benchmarks Report 2025, which analyzed more than 60 million business calls, only 56% of inbound calls are actually answered by a person - meaning nearly half of the people who pick up the phone to call a business just hear it ring (source: invoca.com).

Why an unanswered call is a lost lead, not just an inconvenience

For local and service businesses - lawyers, clinics, contractors - the inbound phone call is still the primary conversion channel, not just a website lead form. Someone who taps "Call" on a Google Business Profile or from mobile search has already decided to reach out to you specifically. The problem is what happens when nobody answers: the caller doesn't leave a patient voicemail and wait, they go back to the search results and call the next number. Voicemail doesn't fix this - it just delays the moment the lead goes to someone else.

What "missed call text-back" actually is in GHL

It's an automation tied to the business phone number connected to GoHighLevel: any inbound call that isn't answered within a preset number of rings fires a trigger, and the system automatically sends an SMS to the caller. The message can vary by business hours - for example, a version that points to a booking link at night, and a version that promises a callback within a set time during work hours. At the same time, the system creates or updates a lead card in the pipeline, so the call doesn't just sit in a phone's call log but becomes an actual follow-up task for someone on the team.

Why this beats "we'll call you back later"

  • Speed is the whole point: a message sent within seconds catches the customer while they're still holding the phone looking for a solution - not tomorrow morning, after they've already booked with someone else.
  • Some customers simply prefer replying by text over a callback - the message gives them an easy way to keep the conversation going without committing to an immediate call.
  • It creates a record: the CRM card opens automatically, so follow-up doesn't depend on the memory of whoever was busy at that exact moment.

What the message actually needs to work

A generic message ("thanks for calling, we'll be in touch soon") isn't enough - it reads as automated and is likely to get zero replies. A message that works acknowledges that the business saw the missed call, and gives the caller one clear, immediate action - a booking link, a WhatsApp number, or a concrete callback time. It's also worth distinguishing a new lead from an existing customer calling in: a message written like a full-on ad doesn't fit someone who already works with you, and that's an easy thing to miss when the automation is set up once and never checked against the call source.

Worth remembering: this is still an automated business SMS, not an exemption from the standard rules. The sending number still needs A2P 10DLC registration, exactly like any other automated business SMS in the US - as we covered in more detail in our article on automated reminders and follow-ups.

When it doesn't replace answering the phone

This automation is a safety net, not an excuse. A call you can answer - you should answer. The automatic text mostly helps in three situations: outside business hours, when several calls come in at once and there aren't enough hands, and during unusual spikes in volume. A business that starts relying on the text to justify being consistently unavailable loses exactly what the automation was meant to prevent - a real human connection at the moment the customer is most open to it.
