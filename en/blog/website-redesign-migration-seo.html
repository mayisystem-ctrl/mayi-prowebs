Redesigning or Migrating Your Website Without Losing Google Rankings - The Technical Steps Google Itself Recommends

Short answer: a website redesign or platform migration doesn't have to cost you organic rankings - the damage happens when URLs change without a one-to-one 301 redirect for every page that was ranking. The real risk isn't the redesign itself, it's what happens under the surface: URL mapping, redirects, and telling Google the site moved rather than disappeared. Google publishes an exact process for this in its own official documentation - and most businesses skip it because it looks "too technical" to spend time on before launch.

Why a site migration is a different kind of SEO risk than a normal content update

Updating a page, adding new content, or tweaking internal design are gradual changes Google crawls and processes without drama. A site migration is fundamentally different: when you change domains, restructure URLs, or move platforms (say, from WordPress to custom code, or from a builder to a new platform), every URL that used to exist can turn into a URL that no longer does. Google doesn't automatically "remember" that a new page is the continuation of an old one - it needs an explicit signal.

Per Google Search Central's official documentation on site moves, changing domains, moving from HTTP to HTTPS, or changing URL path structure are all considered a "site move" from Google's perspective, and all require the same careful process. That page is the starting point for planning any migration or redesign that involves changing URLs.

The step most businesses skip: a full URL map before launch

Before touching the code, the design, or the new platform, the first step is exporting a complete list of every URL that currently exists on the site - including URLs getting organic traffic, URLs with inbound links from other sites, and URLs showing up in Search Console. Every one of those needs a decided-in-advance matching new URL, mapped out in an organized table - not improvised on launch day.

  • Export every ranking URL from Search Console (Performance and Coverage reports).
  • Identify pages with external inbound links - they carry specific SEO trust that has to be preserved.
  • Map one-to-one: every old URL → a specific new URL, not a blanket redirect.
  • Check the content on each destination page - that it genuinely continues the same topic, not just a similar general page.

301 redirects - one-to-one, not "everything to the homepage"

The most common mistake in DIY migrations is redirecting every old URL to the homepage, to "avoid leaving 404 errors." It looks like a safe fix, but to Google it signals that the specific content that was ranking simply disappeared - and the trust built up on that page doesn't transfer anywhere specific. A 301 redirect preserves most of that trust only when it points to a page with genuinely relevant content, not a general one.

How long should the redirects stay up?

Google's Search Advocate John Mueller has explicitly stated it's worth keeping 301 redirects active for at least a year after launch - to give Google's crawler enough passes to recognize the move as permanent and transfer trust accordingly. Removing redirects too early, even after several good months, can reset part of the signal that was already passed (source: searchenginejournal.com).

Updating Search Console and the Change of Address tool

After launch, you need to update the sitemap and resubmit it in Search Console, so Google knows to crawl the new URLs quickly. For a domain migration (not just a URL structure change on the same domain), you also need to use the dedicated Change of Address tool inside Search Console, which explicitly signals the move between properties to Google. Skip that step and Google will still discover the change on its own - but it can take a lot longer.

The opportunity most businesses miss: fixing structure for AI engines too

A redesign is the best opportunity to fix not just Google rankings, but visibility in AI engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity. A study tracking ChatGPT citations found that 44.2% of citations come from the first 30% of a document - meaning content structure matters just as much as the content itself (source: searchengineland.com). If you're rebuilding service pages or articles anyway, this is the moment to make sure the direct answer to each page's core question sits in the opening paragraph - not buried further down.

Why custom code gives you control a builder doesn't

Most builders and landing page platforms cap the number of redirect rules you can set, or don't give you direct access to server configuration files. On a custom-coded site, redirects are defined at the infrastructure level itself - a server or CDN config file - with no rule limit and no dependency on a third-party interface. That's the difference between a fully controlled migration and hoping the platform handles it correctly.
